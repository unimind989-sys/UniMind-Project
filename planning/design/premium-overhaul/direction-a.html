The brachial plexus

The plexus is formed by the ventral rami of C5 to T1. The roots unite to form the upper, middle and lower trunks at the root of the neck.

Injury to the upper trunk (C5, C6) produces Erb–Duchenne paralysis, commonly from excessive separation of the head and shoulder, as in a fall on the shoulder or during a difficult delivery.

The lower trunk (C8, T1) is injured by excessive abduction of the arm, producing Klumpke's paralysis.

Brachial plexus injuries

Started Tuesday

What happens if the upper trunk of the brachial plexus is injured?

Tuesday, 9:40 pm

An upper trunk injury damages the C5 and C6 roots: Erb's palsy. It usually follows a fall on the shoulder or a difficult delivery that pulls the head away from the shoulder.1

The muscles supplied by C5 and C6 weaken: deltoid, supraspinatus, infraspinatus, biceps and brachialis.1 The arm hangs adducted and medially rotated, with the forearm extended and pronated, the "waiter's tip" position.2

Sensation is lost over the lateral arm and forearm.1
